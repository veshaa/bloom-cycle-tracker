Period 101

Understand the basic stages of the menstrual cycle.

Understanding the Menstrual Cycle

The menstrual cycle is a series of changes that prepare the body for a possible pregnancy. Cycle length can vary between individuals and from month to month — this is completely normal.


The 4 Main Phases:
  • 🩸 Menstrual Phase — Uterus lining sheds; bleeding typically lasts 3–7 days.
  • 🌱 Follicular Phase — Body prepares an egg; energy levels often rise.
  • 💜 Ovulation Phase — Egg is released; most fertile time of the cycle.
  • 🌙 Luteal Phase — Body prepares for next period; PMS symptoms may appear.
🌿

Period Cramps

Learn about common menstrual discomfort & how to feel better.

Period Cramps — What Helps?

Menstrual cramps happen when the uterus contracts to help shed its lining. This is caused by chemicals called prostaglandins. Higher levels may mean stronger cramps.


✅ Things That May Help:
  • 🧊 Heat: Hot water bottle or warm towel on your belly
  • 🚶 Movement: Light walking or gentle yoga stretches
  • 💧 Hydration: Drinking water reduces water retention
  • 🛌 Rest: Listen to your body — extra sleep helps
  • 🍵 Teas: Ginger, chamomile, or peppermint may soothe

⚠️ Important: If pain is severe or stops you from daily activities, please see a doctor.

Food & Nutrition

What to eat for energy, mood, and comfort throughout your cycle.

🥗 Eating Well During Your Cycle

Your nutritional needs change throughout the month. Here's a simple guide:


🩸 During Your Period:
  • ✅ Iron-rich foods: Spinach, lentils, red meat, beans — replace what's lost
  • ✅ Warm foods: Soups, stews, ginger tea — comforting and warming
  • ✅ Magnesium: Dark chocolate, bananas, nuts — may reduce cramps
  • ❌ Limit: Salty foods (bloating), too much caffeine (anxiety/mood swings)

💜 Before & After Ovulation:
  • ✅ Complex carbs — steady energy
  • ✅ Healthy fats — avocado, olive oil, nuts
  • ✅ Protein — keeps blood sugar stable

Sleep & Energy

Why your sleep changes and how to rest better.

🌙 Sleep & Your Cycle

Hormone changes directly affect sleep. Many notice deeper sleep after ovulation, and lighter, more restless sleep just before or during their period.


💤 Better Sleep Habits:
  • ⏰ Keep a consistent bedtime — even on weekends
  • 📱 Put screens away 1 hour before bed
  • 🌡️ Keep your room cool — helps deeper sleep
  • ☕ Reduce caffeine after 2 PM
  • 📝 Write worries down before bed — clears your mind

Remember: Feeling tired is your body's signal to slow down — not push harder.

Spotting vs Period

Understand the general difference and when to check in.

Spotting vs Menstrual Bleeding

Spotting means very light bleeding — just a few drops or marks on underwear, not enough to need a full pad or tampon. It can happen:

  • 🌸 At the very start or end of your period
  • 💜 Around ovulation — some light spotting is common
  • 📅 When cycles are adjusting or irregular

Bleeding patterns vary person to person. If spotting happens often or is unusual for you, a healthcare provider can help explain it.

Mood Changes

Why emotions shift and how to support yourself.

😊 Mood & Hormones — It's Real!

Estrogen and progesterone affect brain chemicals like serotonin and dopamine. When these hormones drop before your period, it's completely normal to feel:

  • 😢 More sensitive or tearful
  • 😡 Easily annoyed or frustrated
  • 😟 Anxious or worried
  • 😔 Tired or unmotivated

💛 What Helps:
  • Journal your feelings — getting them out helps
  • Talk to someone you trust — you're not being "too much"
  • Lower expectations — it's okay to do less
  • Do small things you enjoy — music, a walk, a warm drink
📖

Irregular Periods

Why cycles change and when to seek guidance.

Irregular Cycles — What's Normal?

"Regular" doesn't mean exactly the same day every single time. A variation of a few days is usually nothing to worry about.


Common Reasons for Changes:
  • 🧠 Stress — big one! Cortisol can delay ovulation
  • 🏃 Sudden weight change or intense exercise
  • 🥗 Diet changes
  • 😴 Sleep schedule shifts
  • 🩺 New medications or health conditions

⚠️ See a doctor if: Periods are consistently very long, very painful, or absent for months — professional advice helps.

PMS Information

Common premenstrual symptoms and how to manage them.

Premenstrual Symptoms — What Happens?

PMS refers to physical and emotional changes in the days leading up to your period. About 3 out of 4 people who menstruate notice some changes.


Common Signs:
  • 💧 Bloating / water retention
  • 🤕 Breast tenderness
  • 🍫 Food cravings
  • 😤 Mood changes
  • 😴 Feeling tired
  • 🧐 Trouble concentrating

These usually ease once your period starts. If they severely affect your life, speak to a doctor — help is available.
